The fixed veto has plausible adaptive replacements; a single-epoch counterexample does not decide the catalogue mean.

Under the stated budget and nearest-fixture transport, I recommend D(k=0) for the next validation run, not a claim of survey-certified calibration. It retains 1,015 more epochs than the fixed veto, with nominal defect-induced bias far below both bounds. Its size-matched fixture envelope is also inside the budget by factors of about 25 in m and 5 in c. D(k=2) buys additional margin at the cost of another 731 epochs.

Measured: object–epoch masks, fill classification, footprint intersections, relative inverse-variance proxy and rule losses on seven rebuilt exposures. Simulated: conditional m/c, including N=3,5,7 joint fits with one defective epoch. Assumed: transport to real galaxy profiles, nearest fixture/PSF, relative epoch information, equal object weights and additive superposition of multiple defects. No detector-column orientation averaging is used.

Definitions and unknown footprints

  • A: strict <7 px interpolation / <13 px noise epoch veto.
  • B: no central veto, retain all otherwise eligible epochs.
  • C: no central veto; remove the object if any eligible epoch has instrument ISO bits or interpolated ISO pixels. A literal nonzero cut also removes the −1 unknown-footprint sentinel. There are 1,623 positive-flag objects and 623 unknown-footprint objects; their union loses 2,042. A positive-only variant is reported below. Unflagged noise-filled ISO pixels are not captured by these two #973 flags, but none occurred in known eligible footprints here.
  • D: reject an epoch if any production defect intersects the object's segmentation footprint, dilated by an Euclidean disk of radius k=0 or 2 px.
  • E: retain the 13 px noise veto; apply D only to interpolation.

For missing/edge-clipped footprints D/E fall back to A, rather than treating missing information as a clean footprint. The optimistic unknown-footprint variant is shown separately. The coadd segmentation is MegaCam-flipped into each epoch with no reprojection, matching the existing #973 overlay and its limitations.

Multi-epoch dilution is measured, not assumed to be exact

The new joint fits use N=1,2,3,4,5,7 with exactly one defective epoch, the same galaxy in every epoch, equal RMS/PSF, independent subpixel shifts and six paired seeds. Interpolation at 4–6 px is measured on the 0.3″/0.7″ grid and across the other grid points/elliptical PSFs; noise-filled bleeds at 6,8,10,12 px include the decisive 0.7″/0.9″ grid. Additional core-noise cases resolve single-epoch ill-conditioning that would otherwise give B a misleadingly unstable 1/N error bar.

For example, the 0.3″/0.7″ interpolated bleed at 4 px has NΔc1≈4.45×10⁻³ at N=1 but ≈2.17×10⁻³ at N=3 and ≈1.90×10⁻³ at N=7. The 0.7″/0.9″ noise-filled bleed at 10 px has NΔm11≈−6.50% at N=1 and ≈−6.74% at N=3. Thus 1/N can overpredict the first case's additive bias by about a factor two, while working well for the second.

The projection uses measured joint-fit NΔq where a matched multi-epoch fixture exists, choosing the nearest measured effective N=1/α; α is the retained epoch's relative inverse-RMS² information proxy. Elsewhere it uses αΔq_single, explicitly the 1/N/linear-information assumption. Unequal-epoch and multiple-defect interactions are not directly calibrated; equal epoch weights and all-component superposition are sensitivity checks below.

A numerical response outlier is retained, not hidden. The 0.7″/0.9″ interpolated bleed at 4 px, N=2, has Δm11=-0.665 with a 0.349 single-case bootstrap error. One of six seeds has an unflagged metacal response element near 12 instead of 0.94; the other five have near-normal responses. An independent 24-seed repetition (seeds 6–29) gives Δm11=-0.00085 and Δc1=+0.000491. This diagnoses a sparse, zero-fit-flag response failure, not a reproducible −66% physical dilution law. I nevertheless retain the original case in the broad envelope; removing it without a production-quality response criterion would be unjustified.

Adaptive residuals are outside the footprint

Yes: D's retained known-footprint defects are outside that footprint by construction. More specifically, the interpolated 0–7 px outside-footprint contribution to D(k=0)'s catalogue c1 is +6.73×10⁻⁶, versus its total +5.19×10⁻⁶. For k=2, the corresponding contribution is +2.25×10⁻⁶ and the total is +1.52×10⁻⁶. These signed totals have partial cancellation between distance/classes; the envelope below explicitly does not rely on that cancellation.

4 known footprints do not contain the stamp centre. D(k=0) retains 0 epochs with an interpolated central pixel outside the ISO footprint. An adaptive footprint rule is not equivalent to a smaller circular radius. The retained bias here is primarily from near-footprint defects, not a large population of missing central labels.

Model uncertainty and effective density

Both envelopes maximize absolute effective bias over tested geometries, both PSF states, measured N values and neighbouring distance bins, then sum over every component with the measured epoch-information fractions. The size-matched envelope preserves each component's nearest observed-size bin, so it retains the measured size–footprint correlation. The unrestricted envelope also maximizes over all three sizes; it deliberately discards that correlation. Both use no orientation or cross-case sign cancellation and retain the sparse N=2 response outlier. For example, only three large-grid 3–5 px interpolation components survive D(k=0), versus 49 in B; none survive D(k=2). Applying the large-grid outlier to every small-footprint object would overstate that particular correlated scenario. These are empirical fixture scenario envelopes, not confidence intervals or mathematical bounds for arbitrary galaxies/defects.

Recommendation and decision threshold

Pilot D(k=0), keeping A as the unchanged production default until a selected-sample multi-tile injection test. With the nearest-fixture transport specified here, D(k=0)'s nominal mean and size-matched envelope are well inside the stated budget while it rejects 39.6% fewer epochs than A and avoids C's 5.72% object loss. This recommendation uses catalogue dilution and the measured size–footprint relation, not a per-case acceptance threshold. D(k=2) is a more conservative alternative if the extra profile/segmentation margin is needed: its size-matched envelope is smaller, but it saves only 284 epochs versus A and loses five more objects than A. B also has a small nominal mean, so the earlier single-epoch scan did not demonstrate unacceptable survey bias. Its size-matched no-cancellation additive envelope still crosses the target; the unrestricted envelope crosses both targets. The hybrid provides only a modest envelope reduction for 30 extra epoch drops at k=0; interpolation dominates the adaptive residual and I would not retain a fixed noise radius solely on this evidence.

I would choose D(k=2) or the hybrid if actual shear weights, real-mask response tails or field-to-field prevalence consume D(k=0)'s margin. I would choose B if a matched-profile test establishes that the uncut selected-catalogue mean and flags-only policy meet the allocated systematic budget across tiles. A tighter defect budget near 10⁻⁴ in c would also favor k=2. The decisive missing measurement is the selected catalogue's response with real masks, not another comparison of a per-object bias against a catalogue requirement.
