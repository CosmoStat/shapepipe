Star shear-response vs magnitude

The null test extended down to the bright end (Fabian's ask) — all 45,835 stars, not just the 20<mag<26 science window.

A star carries no intrinsic shear, so the measured metacal response R should be ~0. It is — but only for faint, marginally-resolved stars. Toward the bright end the response climbs smoothly to R₁ ≈ 2: bright stars are high-S/N near-point-sources, the metacal response Jacobian dg/dγ goes singular as the pre-PSF size T→0, and R diverges. The 20<mag<26 window (⟨R₁⟩=+0.076) hid most of it.

What it means

This is the point-source limit of metacal, made visible — not a pipeline bug. On a real shear sample these bright stars are removed by a resolution cut (a T/Tpsf floor); they fail it by construction. So:

  • The dashboard shows the full magnitude range (this page) — the bright-end break is the diagnostic, the thing worth seeing.
  • The CI guardrail (#763) asserts the null only where it is physically expected (resolved objects / the science window), since R=0 cannot hold on pathological point sources.
